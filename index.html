Nissan S13

The Icon of 1990s Japanese Drift & Street Culture

Produced by Nissan between 1989 and 1998, the 180SX is a fastback coupe built on the legendary S13 chassis. Sold exclusively in Japan through the Nissan Prince Store network, it became an instant favorite among street racers and grassroots drifters thanks to its front-engine, rear-wheel-drive layout, near 50/50 weight distribution, and signature pop-up headlights.

Why the 180SX Stands Out

Unlike its notchback sibling, the Nissan Silvia, the 180SX features a sleek liftback hatch and retractable headlights across its entire ten year production run. Enthusiasts still praise the chassis today for several reasons:

  • Turbocharged Powerplants: Early models came with the 1.8-litre CA18DET, later upgraded to the famous 2.0-litre SR20DET engine.
  • Lightweight Balance: Weighing roughly 1,220 kg, the chassis responds predictably to throttle and steering inputs.
  • Aftermarket Support: Decades of motorsport heritage mean virtually every suspension, aero, and engine part can still be upgraded.

The Three Production Eras

  1. Zenki (1989–1990): Powered by the 175 hp CA18DET engine with a clean, minimalist front bumper.
  2. Chuki (1991–1995): Introduced the stronger 205 hp SR20DET engine, larger brakes, and updated front styling.
  3. Kouki / Type X (1996–1998): Featured aggressive factory aero, round tail lights, and an iconic rear wing.
